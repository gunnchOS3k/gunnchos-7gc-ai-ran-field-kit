## **1\. System Mission**

**Design, manufacture, deploy, secure, and operate an affordable family of carrier-grade computing devices that provides excellent learning, creation, communication, work, and gaming experiences regardless of where a person was born or what hardware they could previously afford.**

The ecosystem shall:

* Operate as a coherent family rather than isolated products.

* Use the 7 Global Campuses as real-world deployment, measurement, education, and service-continuity environments.  
* Produce reproducible evidence for every claim involving performance, security, accessibility, connectivity, scientific accuracy, or readiness.

## **2\. Important Standards Positioning**

The correct term is **IMT-2030**, not “ITM-2030.”

The ITU has established IMT-2030 as the framework for what is commonly called 6G. Its usage scenarios include ubiquitous connectivity, immersive communication, massive communication, hyper-reliable low-latency communication, integrated sensing and communication, and AI-and-communication capabilities. ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/study-groups/rsg5/rwp5d/imt-2030/pages/default.aspx?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

However, a product built in 2026 cannot yet honestly claim final 6G standards compliance or commercial 6G carrier certification. 3GPP Release 20 contains early 6G studies, while Release 21 is planned as the first normative 6G release. ([3GPP](https://www.3gpp.org/specifications-technologies/releases/release-20?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Therefore, the defensible product language is:

> **5G-Advanced and NTN-capable, IMT-2030-aligned, software-defined, and engineered for migration to standardized 6G.**

The devices should be carrier-grade **today** through reliability, security, fleet management, observability, safe updates, multi-bearer connectivity, and conformance testing—not through premature use of a 6G certification label.

# **4\. Next-Generation I/O Ring Requirements**

## **4.1 Device and Surface Awareness**

The ring system shall determine:

1. Which user is wearing the rings.  
2. Which gunnchOS or authorized third-party device is the intended target.  
3. Which nearby object or surface is being used as the interaction plane.  
4. The surface’s position, orientation, boundaries, and scale.  
5. The user’s hand position relative to that surface.  
6. The intended input event.  
7. The destination application or operating-system service.  
8. Whether the event was confidently recognized.  
9. Whether feedback or correction is required.

A laptop lid, table, notebook, wall, inactive keyboard, desk mat, armrest, or other safe surface may become a **virtual input plane** after registration and calibration.

---

## **4.2 Spatial Input Pipeline**

The required processing chain is:

**Sense → identify user → identify target device → establish spatial frame → detect contact or gesture → infer intended control → validate confidence → transmit securely → render feedback → log only permitted diagnostics**

Potential sensing inputs include:

* Inertial measurement  
* Hand and finger orientation  
* Ultra-wideband ranging  
* Bluetooth channel information  
* Optical or depth sensing  
* Capacitive or pressure sensing  
* Acoustic contact signatures  
* Magnetic sensing  
* Device camera assistance  
* Environmental anchors

No single sensor should be treated as sufficient for every environment.

---

# **7\. Connectivity and the 7 Global Campuses**

> The device continually selects the safest and most capable available communication path, preserves essential service during bearer changes, and continues useful operation when no wide-area bearer exists.

It should not imply that a device can create universal carrier coverage by itself.

Required connectivity paths may include:

* 5G and 5G-Advanced  
* Future standardized 6G  
* Wi-Fi  
* Private cellular  
* Satellite or other NTN access  
* Device-to-device communication  
* Local mesh  
* Community edge networks  
* Ethernet through docks  
* Delay-tolerant networking  
* Store-and-forward synchronization  
* Emergency low-bandwidth modes

3GPP already standardized initial NR and IoT NTN support in Release 17 and enhanced NTN capabilities in Release 18, making NTN a credible near-term foundation while 6G develops. ([3GPP](https://www.3gpp.org/technologies/ntn-overview?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Every scientific record requires:

* Canonical identifier  
* Scientific name  
* Taxonomic authority  
* Source organization  
* Source record identifier  
* License  
* Retrieval date  
* Version  
* Geographic provenance  
* Time range  
* Confidence or uncertainty  
* Editorial status  
* Citation shown to the user where appropriate

No source should be marked integrated without real imported and audited data. The current notes already require honest blocked states when source snapshots, downloads, or credentials are unavailable.

# **10\. Definition of “Fully Operational”**

The ecosystem is fully operational only when all of these layers are complete together:

1. **Industrial design** — enclosure, ergonomics, thermal behavior, ports, durability.  
2. **Electrical hardware** — boards, power, radios, antennas, displays, sensors, security components.  
3. **Firmware** — boot chain, controllers, battery management, radio firmware, ring firmware.  
4. **Operating system** — gunnchOS drivers, services, security, updates, recovery, accessibility.  
5. **AI platform** — gunnchAI3k local and cloud orchestration, evaluation, safety, governance.  
6. **Connectivity** — terrestrial, Wi-Fi, private network, NTN, mesh, offline continuity.  
7. **Input ecosystem** — touch, keyboard, mouse, controllers, voice, rings, accessibility devices.  
8. **Applications** — learning, communication, creation, productivity, management.  
9. **Games** — complete loops, performance, controls, saves, multiplayer, accessibility.  
10. **Cloud and edge services** — identity, synchronization, matchmaking, telemetry, content delivery.  
11. **Security operations** — monitoring, incident response, patching, disclosure, recovery.  
12. **Manufacturing** — sourcing, assembly, provisioning, quality control, traceability.  
13. **Certification** — radio, electrical, safety, environmental, carrier and regional requirements.  
14. **Deployment** — logistics, activation, training, connectivity, fleet management.  
15. **Support** — repair, documentation, warranty, replacement, data recovery, end-of-life.  
16. **Evidence** — test reports, source provenance, field measurements, reproducible demonstrations.

A rendered prototype, dev-board assembly, APK, simulation, concept image, or passing unit test can satisfy part of a gate. None alone proves a fully operational product.
